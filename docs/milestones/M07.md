## The headline: §27's duplication rule, read literally, made the scheduler worse than random

The scheduler benchmark gives every allocation strategy the **same fixed episode budget** over the
same task pool and counts how many distinct tasks get solved. Everything but the strategy is held
fixed.

The first run said reasoned allocation **lost to random** at every budget above 9:

| episode budget | random | reasoned strategies | gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | 0.222 | 0.389 | +0.167 |
| 9 | 0.556 | 0.445 | **−0.111** |
| 12 | 0.667 | 0.500 | **−0.167** |
| 16 | 0.944 | 0.833 | **−0.111** |

Chasing it produced the milestone's real finding. Repeated attempts on a single task, each seeing
what the last recorded:

| attempt | succeeded | probes | duplicate failures surfaced |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | no | 6 | 0 |
| 2 | no | 2 | 4 |
| 3 | **yes** | 1 | 2 |
| 4 | yes | 0 | 0 |

Attempts 1 and 2 failed **but recorded which operations were ruled out**, and attempt 3 succeeded
on that record. Random allocation re-attempted tasks and so accumulated that negative knowledge;
the reasoned strategies spread episodes across tasks and never built it.

§27 says *avoid wasting agents on identical duplicated work*. Read literally, the duplication
penalty punishes exactly the behaviour that makes a collective work: **a repeat that consumes what
the last repeat left behind is continuation, not duplication.**

`duplication_penalty` now takes `productive_attempts` and penalises only repetition that left
nothing for a successor. An attempt that created an artifact or documented a failure does not
count against its task. The same sweep after the correction:

| episode budget | random | reasoned strategies | gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | 0.333 | 0.444 | **+0.111** |
| 9 | 0.389 | 0.556 | **+0.167** |
| 12 | 0.555 | 0.833 | **+0.278** |
| 16 | 0.944 | 0.944 | 0.000 (both saturated) |

The sign is reversed at every budget below saturation, and the correction is causally attributed:
one function changed, everything else held.

### A limitation, stated rather than glossed

**The four reasoned strategies score identically at every budget.** The task pool is symmetric —
equal difficulty, no subproblems, no contradictions, one profile — so `priority`, `uncertainty`,
`information_gain` and `cost_aware` all reduce to ordering by unproductive attempts. This benchmark
can distinguish *reasoning about allocation* from *not reasoning about it*; it cannot distinguish
the strategies from each other. Doing that needs a heterogeneous pool, which is M9's work.

## Specialization is measured, never declared (§26)

`AgentProfile.performance_by_work_type` is a fold over evaluations, written only by
`specialization.update_profiles`. A test writes a fabricated `{"n": 999, "success_rate": 1.0}` onto
a profile and asserts the measurement replaces it.

Three guards against the obvious trap — a profile that drew two easy tasks looking like a
specialist:

- every rate carries its sample size, and `confident` is false below five episodes;
- `best_profile_for` returns `None` rather than a guess when nothing qualifies, so the caller
  explores instead of entrenching an accident;
- `_profile_fit` returns 0.5 for an unmeasured pairing — unknown is not bad, and treating it as
  bad would freeze allocation on whichever profile was tried first.

**Infrastructure failures do not count against a profile.** A flaky provider must not look like a
weak specialist.

The **specialization index** is a normalised Herfindahl over per-work-type success shares, and it
returns `None` — not 0.0 — when fewer than two profiles have worked any work type. One profile
cannot specialise, and reporting zero would present "there is nobody to divide labour with" as
"labour is not divided".

## Deliberate disagreement is a runtime configuration, not a persona (§28)

The six roles §28 names are `AgentProfile` rows whose *tool policy* and *retrieval policy* differ,
enforced by the runtime rather than requested in a prompt a model may ignore.

- **replicator** — its retrieval **excludes `conclusion`, `result` and `answer` types**. An agent
  shown the answer it is meant to independently confirm is agreeing, not replicating, and the
  `reproduces` edge it writes would be worthless while looking like the strongest evidence in the
  graph. The exclusion is recorded on the `RetrievalDecision` as `role_excluded_type`, not merely
  applied.
- **critic** — ranks validation, provenance and confidence *negatively*. A critic shown the
  best-supported artifacts first is pointed away from what is most likely wrong.
- **adversary**, **alternative_hypothesis**, **verifier**, **synthesizer** each carry their own
  weights.

Seeding roles never overwrites `performance_by_work_type`: that column is a measurement, and
re-seeding must not erase what the collective learned.

### The hypothesis state machine (§28)

A closed transition graph over all eight states. Nothing jumps from `proposed` to `accepted`
because an agent said so, and `advance` walks the ladder one legal step at a time so the record
shows the path a claim actually took — a hypothesis that went `proposed → accepted` in one write
would be indistinguishable from one that had been tested, supported and replicated.

Three decisions worth naming:

- **`accepted` is not terminal.** A record that could not withdraw an accepted claim could not
  self-correct (§11).
- **A self-reproduction does not count as replication.** `independent_replications` counts distinct
  episodes *other than the claim's author*; a claim confirmed by the episode that produced it is
  confirmed by its author.
- **Verification scales with what depends on a claim, not with its author's confidence.**
  Confidence is the one input an agent controls, and letting it set the bar would let a claim lower
  its own standard of proof.

### Subproblems are not tasks (§27)

`decompose` records subproblems the scheduler tracks. Making them tasks would commit an episode to
each before anyone judged it worth one; keeping them separate lets the scheduler weigh them against
everything else first. A test asserts decomposition creates no tasks.

**None.** +0.300, unchanged — and structurally so: that benchmark allocates one episode per class
uniformly and never consults the scheduler, so allocation cannot affect it.

This is the third consecutive milestone whose feature the newcomer benchmark cannot see, and the
three nulls have different causes worth keeping separate: M5's was *corpus too small and queries
too exact*; M6's was *the task needs no tool*; M7's is *the benchmark does not use the component
at all*. Each was diagnosed and each produced its own instrument. That is the pattern M9 has to fix
at the source.

## §23 — Distributed knowledge: the collective solves what no episode can

The device has a **two-factor rule**: each class belongs to a hidden family, each family accepts a
hidden operation, and the answer is their composition. `probe_family` goes to partition A,
`probe_table` to partition B, and **the integrator gets neither**.

`assert_unsolvable_alone` **proves** the property before the benchmark runs, by enumerating what
each partition could learn with unlimited probing. A distributed-cognition result on a task that
turned out to be individually solvable would be evidence of nothing, and this is cheaper than
discovering that afterwards. A test also constructs a degenerate device and checks the proof
*fails* on it.

| arm | integrator success |
|---|---|
| **both partitions** | **0.667** |
| partition A only | 0.000 |
| partition B only | 0.250 |
| no partitions (floor) | 0.250 |
| scrambled | 0.167 |

- **gain over the best single partition: +0.417**
- gain over the floor: +0.417 · gain over scrambled: +0.500

The single-partition arms are the load-bearing controls. They hold the quantity of *relevant*
knowledge far above the floor while remaining provably insufficient, so an integrator that beats
both is combining information rather than exploiting either half. Partition A alone scores 0.000 —
knowing every class's family tells you nothing about operations, exactly as the proof says.

## §24 — Cumulative culture: depth reached only through inheritance

The chain is literal, and the dependency is on **which question to ask**:

    class(n) = classes[ sha256(result(n−1)) mod |classes| ]

An agent in generation 3 that cannot find generation 2's result does not know which class to probe,
so it cannot start — not merely start slower.

| arm | gen 1 | gen 2 | gen 3 | gen 4 | deepest reached |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **chained** | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | **4** |
| broken chain | 1.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 | **1** |

**Depth gain: 3 generations. Success-rate gain: +0.75.**

The broken-chain control runs the identical generations with the identical agents and budgets and
empties the workspace between them. Generation 1 succeeds in *both* arms, which is what makes it a
control rather than a handicap: only the availability of the prerequisite differs.

## §25 — Capability frontier: individual plateaus, collective rises

Difficulty is the number of candidate operations; the model, prompts, tools and budgets are
identical at every difficulty and in every arm (§20). Frontier = the largest difficulty at which an
arm still clears a 0.75 success rate. n = 16 per point.

| arm | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 | 14 | 16 | frontier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| solo | 0.750 | 0.562 | 0.500 | 0.625 | 0.562 | 0.250 | **6** |
| independent | 0.750 | 0.562 | 0.500 | 0.625 | 0.562 | 0.250 | **6** |
| shared_memory | 0.250 | 0.250 | 0.312 | 0.375 | 0.375 | 0.062 | **none** |
| **collective** | 1.000 | 0.938 | 0.875 | 0.750 | 0.750 | 0.562 | **14** |

This is §25's target phenomenon: *individual capability reaches a plateau; collective capability
continues rising*, with the individual model held exactly constant.

Two readings of the table matter as much as the headline:

**`independent` tracks `solo` exactly.** That is the internal validity check, and it is the one
that would have invalidated everything. `independent` is §21's brute-force condition — many agents,
no transmission. If it had matched `collective`, the advantage would be *volume* rather than
transmission and the whole claim would be unsupported. It matches `solo` at every difficulty, so
the collective's gain is not repeated sampling.

**`shared_memory` never clears the bar, and underperforms `solo`.** The conventional curated
shared-memory condition returns only validated, accepted results — and in this workspace the
accumulated findings are self-reported, so it sees almost nothing while still paying the search
cost. Reported rather than smoothed over: **a curated memory is worse than no memory when nothing
has been curated**, which is a real property of the arm as §21 specifies it and a real limitation
of curation as a strategy for a young collective.

A frontier an arm never clears is reported as `None`, not as the smallest difficulty tested.
"Never cleared the bar" is not a frontier of zero.

## Two defects the measurements exposed

1. **The first cumulative design measured nothing.** Every arm read 0.000 at every generation. The
   budget was one tool call short — a generation-1 agent found the answer on its final probe and
   had nothing left to record or submit with. The deeper problem was that the inheritance only
   reordered the search, so it was an optimisation rather than a dependency. Redesigned so the
   prerequisite determines *which question is being asked*.
2. **The first frontier run gave every arm a frontier of 14.** The threshold was 0.5 and n was 8,
   so noise carried `solo` over the bar at every difficulty. The same ceiling effect the M4 sweep
   documented: an instrument has to be checked for resolution before its output is read.
